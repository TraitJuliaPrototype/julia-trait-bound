## Summary

The plan's direction is right: one question per method at the new set and its supersets, against the method table at the member, subsumes the pairs of sets and finds the cases the pair loop misses. Four of them I verified as missed today and ambiguous at the call: a tie across argument positions with a method at a closed declared supertype (B), the order case `struct S <: X; @implement Y S` (K), a set the member is in through a pattern entry on its declared supertype (F), and a superset's method at a set-in-set membership (E). The cost is small: 0.1 ms per membership on average, under 1 ms at worst, under 5 ms for a port's whole load.

But the plan as written is wrong in three places, one of them fatal to TraitWorld's own load under strict mode:
- **At a pattern or set member, "any match in the method table" includes methods reached only through a set the member is not in.** `getindex(::Keyed)` matches `getindex(MutableIndexable, Any)` at `All{Keyed, MutableIndexable}`, which the runtime counts as met (the `Dynamic{Z}` patterns make every pair of traits met). Under the plan's rule the supertrait declaration `@trait MutableIndexable <: Indexable` and three `Dynamic{Z}` memberships of BaseTraits are findings, so strict mode refuses TraitWorld's own memberships at its precompile. The reference's own scoping ("another set the member is in, or a declared supertype") must stay in the match filter (H1).
- **The plan's gate asserts a warning where there is no ambiguity.** `f(::A)` against `f(::X)` at `struct S <: A` is no tie: a closed type beats a set (§5.2), `f(S())` dispatches to `f(::A)`. The C-78 row's example (`f(::AbstractString)` against `f(::X)`) is the same non-case. The real declared-supertype case is a tie across positions (H2).
- **The substitution skips a method at `All{X, Y}`** (and at a union or a variable's bound), so the joint method that resolves one pair, becoming applicable at a third set's member, is missed today and under the plan (G) (H3).

Below those: "created by the membership" is not in the rule (M1); a tie with a method at `Any` is a table-level ambiguity that strict mode would now refuse at every membership of the set (M2); the joint signature of three tied methods is their common intersection, and one finding per function loses joints (M3); the gates and records the plan forgets (M4).

## Findings, by severity

### H1. The search at an abstract member reports ties through sets the member is not in; strict mode then refuses TraitWorld's own memberships — verified (`probe_cost.jl` second pass, `probe_unmet.jl`)

- The plan's rule (§2): "a match `m` with `mX` both covering `q`'s intersection, neither more specific, and no match more specific than both that covers it." For a concrete member `q` is a dispatch tuple and every match is at the member itself. For a pattern or set member `q` is abstract, and `ml_matches_i` returns every method that intersects it, a method at a set the member is not in included, at the intersection `All{member, Y}` (§5.3).
- At TraitTables' and TraitCSV's loads the probe found four memberships of BaseTraits whose set's method is tied, at such an intersection, with a method at a third set: `MutableIndexable` in `Indexable` (the supertrait declaration; `q = getindex(MutableIndexable, Any)`, `getindex(::Indexable{T}, ::Any)` tied with `getindex(::Keyed{K, V}, ::Any)` at `All{Keyed, MutableIndexable}`), and the `Dynamic{Z}` memberships of `Indexable`, `MutableIndexable` and `Keyed` (trait.jl:107 declares one per trait). `Base.isambiguous(getindex(::Indexable), getindex(::Keyed))` is `true` today; `jl_has_unmet_argument(Tuple{typeof(getindex), All{Keyed, MutableIndexable}, Any})` answers *met*, while `jl_candidates_of(Keyed)` is empty and no type is in both. Reasoning on why: `set_entries_meet` (datatype.c:859–885) asks each entry's member pattern against the other components, and `Dynamic{Z} where Z <: Keyed` meets `Dynamic{Z} where Z <: MutableIndexable` as a type expression. So the met test is conservative, and `isambiguous`'s filter (reflection.jl:1206, 1217) does not save the plan.
- Under the plan's rule these four are findings. Under strict mode (`JULIA_TW_STRICT=1`, which reaches a precompile process: the T-98 review M3) the `@trait MutableIndexable{T} <: Indexable{T}` declaration and three `@trait` declarations' `Dynamic` memberships are refused, and TraitWorld does not precompile. Today's pair loop does not see them: it pairs only the sets of the member's own entry list (gf.c:8374–8395), and `Keyed` is not among them.
- The reference already scopes the report: "a method at another set the member is in, or at one of the member's declared supertypes" (§5.4). The plan drops the scoping together with the pair loop.
- **Change:** a match `m` counts at `q` only where its coverage at the member's positions does not pass through a set the member is not in. Concretely, at each position where `q` holds the member, `m`'s type there must be one the member is under in the entry's world (`jl_subtype(member, p_m)`, the member's variables existential), or, finer, the intersection of the member and `p_m` must name no `All` whose set component the member is not in. For a concrete member nothing changes (a concrete non-member meets no method at a set, §4.7). The other arguments stay as they are: a tie at a narrower other argument (`h2(::XE, ::Int)` against `q = h2(ME, Integer)`) is real. Gates: TraitWorld's own test suite and the ports' loads under `JULIA_TW_STRICT=1` pass with no refusal; a gate with a set-in-set declaration `@trait Z <: X` and a method at an unrelated set `Y` with a `Dynamic`-like pattern member in each: no warning, no refusal.

### H3. The substitution skips a method at `All{X, Y}`, a union or a variable's bound; the joint method as a source of a new tie is missed — verified (probe G, `probe_skipped.jl`)

## The questions, answered

1. **Right and complete?** Right for a concrete member, with H3's substitution: it finds B, E, F, G, K and the varargs case (J, which today's loop finds too), and nothing at the non-cases A, H, I. Not right for a pattern or set member without H1's scoping: it reports ties through sets the member is not in, and the met test cannot filter them. Not complete for a method named only inside an invariant parameter (a stated limit). "Only those": a dominating method is handled by coverage (the suppress case holds); an ambiguity that existed before is excluded only by M1's rule. Supersets by entries belong in the search (E is missed today) and `membership_superset_sets` finds them, as the plan says.
2. **Subsumes the pair loop?** Yes, once H1's scoping is in the match filter and H3's substitution covers `All` and unions; then the loop goes. The findings differ: one per `mX` and tied set instead of one per function and pair, with the joint of all tied signatures (M3); no re-report of an old pair at an unrelated membership (M1, a benefit today's strict mode lacks); a changed message and `found` shape (M3); the pattern path's suppression loop replaced by coverage (L3).
3. **Cost:** L1. Small at every port measured; not measured for TraitMakie and TDE.
4. **Forgotten; a simpler alternative?** M4. The plan's rejected alternatives are rightly rejected. There is no simpler principled form than one lookup per method the membership makes applicable, with the matches scoped to the member; M1's "did not apply before" is the definition of "created by the membership" the reference lacks.
