# W-104: the ports on the unified build, in slices

Status: **adopted** (2026-11-07, mine, under the user's "Let's revisit this later. Do no regret work now. Building the ports is definitely sensible"). Not normative. The rules are the references'. Choices below are mine, recorded in [the decision log](../record/decisions.md) §9.

## 1. The order

The overarching performance goal is a first Makie + DataFrames workload as fast as its Python equivalent (decisions §9, the user, 2026-11-07). So the DataFrames side and the plotting core come first, and SciML last:

1. **Slice 1, the small cores:** TraitTables, TraitCSV, TraitMakie, and TraitReport's trait form (CSV to PNG). It is the shape that matched pandas and matplotlib on the earlier build: 0.63 against 0.61–0.62 s ([evidence §3](../design/evidence.md#3-first-calls-and-sessions), E-233).
2. **Slice 2, the forks:** Tables, CSV, DataFrames. The earlier build ran them at 7–9× pandas (E-233).
3. **Slice 3, SciML:** TraitDifferentialEquations, and NetworkDynamics' port.

## 2. How

- Each port lives on a branch `unified` in a sibling worktree, `<Repo>-unified`, its `[sources]` pointing at `TraitWorld-unified`. The branch `main` stays for the earlier build, so that the prototype's shapes can be compared (E-271).
- The demonstrators follow the design: a construct of the earlier design is rewritten in the current one. A rewrite the current design makes awkward is recorded as an open issue, with the case, the rule that blocks it and a proposal; the port is not bent around it.

## 3. Gates, per slice (the todo row W-104)

- The port's own tests pass on the unified build.
- Results are identical to plain Julia's: the baselines PlainTables, PlainPlots, PlainReport.
- The session against [evidence §3](../design/evidence.md#3-first-calls-and-sessions), registered as an experiment before it runs (E-284 for slice 1).
